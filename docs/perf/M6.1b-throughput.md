memory, swap already full), 2026-10-30, under `nice -n 10`, one run at a time. Same harnesses,
workload and worker settings as [M6.1](M6.1-performance.md) (campaign 8 × 10, SMTP
`max_conns` 10, tx 2/s, 300 s windows), except for the event rotation below. Data directories

## Changes

**Outbox** (`src/messaging/sends.ts`, `src/messaging/outbox.ts`). Before, every message took
three FULL-sync commits: the claim, `admit`'s `skip_reason` update (autocommit), and the
outcome in `finish` (autocommit), and the loop sent one message at a time.

- `sends.claimBatch` claims up to 10 due rows per commit with the existing lowest-id claim, one
  row after another, and runs each row's admission (payload decode, eligibility, consent and
  cap, dropped copies) right after its claim, in the same transaction. Skips, payload failures
  and the copy count therefore commit with the claim, before any SMTP call. Because each row is
  admitted right after its own claim, a later marketing row in the batch sees the earlier one as
  `sending` and is capped, exactly as when it was claimed one loop iteration later.
- The batch's messages are pushed in parallel on the pool. When every push has settled,
  `sends.finishAll` journals all outcomes in one commit, with the unchanged per-row `finish`
  rules (D24 retry only for proven transient transactional rejections, deadlines 1/2/4/8 s from
  the injected clock, at most five attempts; marketing never retries; ambiguous is `unknown`).
  A push that rejects leaves its row `sending` and fails the outbox, as before.
- The synchronous tx dispatch (`claimQueued`) claims and admits all its rows in one commit the
  same way.
- Recovery, shutdown order and the claim order are unchanged. A kill anywhere between the claim
  commit and the outcome commit leaves the whole batch `sending`, which startup recovery turns
  into `unknown`; nothing is reset to `queued`.
- Per outbox message: 3 commits → 2/10 commits at a full batch. No partial retry index was
  added: the retry half of the claim was not a measured cost in these runs.

**Campaign pacing** (`src/campaigns/runner/runner.ts`, D59). Each worker keeps the times of its
last `app.message_rate` pushes. Once it has `rate` of them, it waits only until one second has
passed since the oldest, instead of a full second after reaching the rate. A push's time is
taken after any sliding-window hold. This is a sliding-log reading of D59's "end of the
current second": a fixed window restarted at the first message could let a late burst at the
end of one window and an early one at the start of the next put up to 2 × rate in one second;
the sliding log cannot. Concurrency, the sliding window and the error threshold are unchanged.
The new runner test (`D59 pacing never exceeds the per-worker rate in any one-second window`)
uses 0–270 ms of varying SMTP time at 1 × 4: no one-second window, starting or ending at any
push, holds more than 4, and 41 messages take 10.0–10.5 s. On the previous code the same test
takes 15.4 s and fails its upper bound.

**Harness** (`test/perf/m61/mixed.ts`). Event *n* still goes to subscriber `n % 2000`, but the
signup slot shifts by one per pass over the subscribers:
`signup = (n + floor(n / 2000)) % 10 === 0`. Signups stay 10% (3,000 in 300 s), and each
signup subscriber gets `order_paid` events on later passes, so `nudge` sees real `exit_on`
exits. The run records enrollments by sequence, status and `exit_reason` at the window end
(`event:signup` is a `reentry: always` replacement, `event:order_paid` an `exit_on` exit).
`test/perf/m61/outbox.ts` also times the batched claim (10 claims with admission), rolled back.

## Results against the targets

- **Campaign 50/s sustained under mixed load: met.** 78.8/s mean, no 10 s bucket below
  77.7/s, at the same 8 × 10 settings that gave 48.9/s (M6.1) and 50.2/s with a 40.0/s worst
  bucket (before, rotated harness). The campaign now runs at its configured 80/s ceiling.
  Without sequences and tx it runs at 79.7/s.
- **Events at 100/s: throughput met, latency not met.** All 30,000 events completed at 100/s
  with nothing shed in every run. Latency is still above the M5.1 baseline: after p50 / p95 /
  p99 of 22.2 / 51.3 / 66.6 ms against the events-only reference measured today, 6.3 / 10.5 /
  14.5 ms (M5.1: 6.8 / 12.1 / 18.0 ms). The same run on the before code gave 17.7 / 46.0 /
  61.2 ms while the campaign sent 50/s rather than 79/s.
- **Outbox:** the drain went from 83–89/s to 498–515/s, and tx delivery p95 under mixed load
  stayed at 165–178 ms (M6.1: 639 ms).
- **Sequences:** every signup's `PT0S` send was delivered, and real `exit_on` exits are now part
  of the measured load.
- **RSS** stayed at or below 119 MiB in the server runs and 136 MiB in the in-process drain;
  MemAvailable stayed at or above 7.2 GiB. **No server errors** in any run.

## Remaining bottleneck

Event latency under mixed load no longer depends on the campaign rate: at 8 × 7 (55/s) the
event p95 was 50.3 ms, at 8 × 10 (79/s) 51.3 ms. Without sequences and tx, at 80/s, it is
22.2 ms. That points to the sequence and event write path as the source of the added delay, not
the campaign or the outbox (an inference from these runs, not a profile). All of it runs on the one JavaScript thread, and each FULL-sync
commit blocks it for about 4–5 ms (M5.1 probe). Per signup (10/s) the thread commits the
event group, two enrollment starts and, for `welcome`, a replacement. The scheduler then commits
its decision and send row, and the outbox its claim and outcome. Each `order_paid` to a
`nudge` subscriber commits an exit. This task cut the outbox's share, but the rest is
outside its authorized changes:

1. **Write coalescing across loops** (storage, D19/D22): one shared group-commit point for
   event, scheduler and outbox writes, at the cost of a small per-write delay.
2. **Scheduler batching** (sequences owner): decide several due enrollments per commit, as the
   outbox now claims several sends.
3. `synchronous=NORMAL` in WAL would remove most of the fsync cost but changes durability
   and needs an owner decision. It is not proposed here.

Event latency is the remaining unmet part of the target. No test or workload was changed to
hide it.

## Crash and acceptance evidence

`bun run sends:batch-crash` (`test/sends/batch-crash.ts`) runs the production outbox in a
child process over 25 queued sequence sends and kills it with SIGKILL:

- **claimed:** after the batch claim committed, while the first SMTP connection had not yet
  received a greeting. 10 rows `sending`, nothing at SMTP. After restart: those 10 are
  `unknown`/`interrupted` with one attempt and never reached SMTP; the other 15 were sent once.
- **mid-batch:** after three messages of the batch were accepted and a fourth final dot was
  held. After restart: all 10 claimed rows are `unknown` (including the three accepted ones,
  whose outcome commit was lost). None reached SMTP again, and the other 15 were sent once.

The full acceptance list and its results are in the PR description.

The first attempt of the after baseline run hit the 590 s command timeout (seeding 27,401
subscribers plus the 300 s window plus the build), while another session's test suite was using
5.7 GiB on the host. It wrote no record, and its data directory was removed. The run was
repeated with a prebuilt executable once the host was quiet; that is the reported row.
