# M2.2 outbox measurements

These are bounded component observations from 2026-10-29, not a throughput
claim, a provider-delivery guarantee, or a CI threshold. Linux x86-64 kernel
6.12.113+deb13-amd64, Bun 1.4.4, AMD Ryzen 5 1600 (six logical CPUs),
nodemailer 10.2.1 and Mailpit 1.32.0. Both databases and TMPDIR were on ext4,
with the production SQLite WAL and synchronous=FULL settings. Runs were serial;
the host was not reserved exclusively.

The control was rebuilt from the exact git-archive export of M2.1 merge
`542bf92c308233971a3c9956f024cf702c6ec1d2`, under the ignored
`node_modules/.cache` subtree, with frozen install and build. Its executable
SHA-256 was `d6f971cd353eb7323d845e5e1c915e8bdadc99a54c0526e37a759ede2bd764fa`.
The measured candidate runtime source was
`261e6bfee589b1d124d162b1716538aacf4c43bf`; its compiled executable SHA-256 was
`ad8462c502b3f2be13fc393f2d43c7f1ce4ef30bfaf66ebb745fe2dcf2ddd870`.
The SMTP workload imports each actual owner; the common HTTP workload uses the
compiled executables. No production data or credentials were used.

## Direct and durable SMTP owners

`TMPDIR=<owned-ext4-dir> bun run reference:run -- bun test/sends/measure.ts`
uses one producer, a one-connection pool and the invocation-owned relay and
labelled Mailpit. Both modes send the same synthetic plain messages, one at a
time, and wait for SMTP acceptance. The durable mode also commits enqueue,
claim and terminal status before the next message; queue occupancy is bounded
to one. Each mode warms up ten messages and then runs three serial batches of
50. Wall time excludes Mailpit observation/removal. Enqueue is the mean time
for the durable enqueue call; connections are newly created within that batch,
with pooled connections retained across batches.

| Mode | Run | Accepted / matching IDs | New connections / peak live | Wall ms | Mean enqueue ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M2.1 direct | 1 | 50 / 50 | 0 / 1 | 146.185 | — |
| M2.1 direct | 2 | 50 / 50 | 1 / 1 | 127.007 | — |
| M2.1 direct | 3 | 50 / 50 | 0 / 1 | 116.455 | — |
| M2.2 durable | 1 | 50 / 50 | 0 / 1 | 522.056 | 1.703 |
| M2.2 durable | 2 | 50 / 50 | 1 / 1 | 470.359 | 1.239 |
| M2.2 durable | 3 | 50 / 50 | 0 / 1 | 456.364 | 1.341 |

Warmups took 67.431 ms direct and 100.072 ms durable; each used one new
connection, had ten acceptances and ten matching IDs. The durable path was
slower in every measured batch, as expected from its extra durable writes.
These short runs do not isolate claim and terminal-commit latency, measure
larger queue occupancy, or establish performance under competing load. No
retries occurred. The harness removed its own messages, closed its pools and
relay, and removed its temporary database. The reference runner cleaned its
own services.

The separately compiled producer's six explicit kill barriers demonstrate
bounded one-row claims and conservative recovery through production journal
routes and Mailpit. Queued work sent once after restart; claimed, DATA and
acknowledged-but-uncommitted work became unknown; committed work stayed sent.
The DATA and held-final-ACK cases use an explicitly injected **fixture-only**
marketing policy. These are correctness observations, not recovery-time or
production marketing-policy measurements. Independent timing was not included in this initial run; see the final remeasurement below.

## Final runtime remeasurement

The earlier values above are retained as the initial observations, including
456–522 ms durable versus 116–146 ms direct. They were measured before the
final read-query and media-type changes. The final runtime source commit is
`5b8f0e04694d71bd6a70641ab13f63590500d8c8`, and its compiled executable
SHA-256 is `a2141582715b7929e6385ac753b14b0ef98de0affa81877489b5185cd70560e3`.
Subsequent changes are acceptance harness, CI and documentation only. The
exact M2.1 control was rebuilt in this worktree's test-excluded cache and again
matched `d6f971cd353eb7323d845e5e1c915e8bdadc99a54c0526e37a759ede2bd764fa`.
The same host, ext4 temporary directory, SQLite FULL/WAL, serial workload,
warmups and counts described above were used. [Raw credential-free output](M2.2-final-raw.md)
contains all repetitions and sample summaries.

| SMTP mode | Warmup ms | Batch 1 ms | Batch 2 ms | Batch 3 ms | Mean enqueue per batch ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| M2.1 direct | 71.376 | 136.586 | 162.991 | 104.420 | — |
| M2.2 durable | 106.827 | 489.196 | 449.398 | 442.969 | 1.800 / 1.283 / 1.375 |

Each measured batch accepted 50 messages with 50 matching IDs, at most one
live connection, and 0/1/0 new connections across the three batches. Ten
warmup messages also matched. This includes durable journal writes and is
slower in these observations; it is not a throughput or delivery guarantee.

A separately compiled test harness surrounded concrete owner calls on three
fresh databases, serially. It enqueued a finite backlog of 32, measured the
whole synchronous enqueue call (32 samples), and measured `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`,
claim and commit together (32 samples). It then closed and reopened the DB,
constructed the owners and ran recovery with one interrupted claim. Recovery
timing runs from opening the database through the recovery write; it excludes
process startup and network. The remaining 31 rows were sent serially to
Mailpit, and the terminal owner write was timed separately (31 samples). One
unknown and 31 sent projections, plus 31 matching received IDs, were observed
in each run. This owner harness is not a production process restart timing.

| Run | Enqueue mean ms | Build queue ms | Claim/commit mean ms | Terminal mean ms | Reopen/recovery ms | DB + WAL growth bytes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.585 | 50.966 | 1.427 | 1.563 | 6.681 | 1,083,560 |
| 2 | 1.643 | 52.735 | 1.527 | 1.623 | 4.823 | 1,083,560 |
| 3 | 1.105 | 35.483 | 1.220 | 1.381 | 3.509 | 1,083,560 |

Growth is the difference in DB plus WAL file sizes immediately before and
after enqueue, not payload-only storage or a steady-state disk forecast.
Owner construction, OS cache and checkpoint behavior affect the samples. The
production process recovery and shutdown checks are correctness tests, not
these timings. No unbounded backlog or concurrent benchmark was run.

Three additional serial process-restart samples used the separately compiled
`test/sends/measure-restart.ts` harness and producer on the same ext4 FULL/WAL
setup. Each fresh database was killed after the durable claim and before SMTP.
The stopwatch starts immediately before spawning the replacement process and
stops after its outbox startup and authenticated HTTP detail reports the
recovered unknown row. The samples were **423.765, 443.698 and 485.011 ms**;
each retained one attempt and its Message-ID. These include process startup,
authentication fixture setup, database/owner construction, recovery and HTTP,
with no warmup; they are not isolated recovery-write latency or a production
executable startup benchmark. No SMTP was attempted in these samples. The
actual production executable's recovery is verified separately for correctness.
