## Behavior

1. **Analytics screens:** campaign and list analytics with upstream's date ranges, types, charts and link tables, reading the M3.3 routes. A 429, 422 or 500 from the reader shows as a message.
2. **Logs:** `GET /api/logs` with `settings:get`, from a bounded in-memory buffer of recent application logs. The screen shows them as text. Nothing secret or personal reaches this buffer: check what the app logs and keep tokens, passwords and addresses out, since `settings:get` is not administrator.
3. **Maintenance API:** `DELETE /api/maintenance/subscribers/:type` (`orphan`, `blocklisted`), `DELETE /api/maintenance/analytics/:type` (`all`, `subscribers`, with `before_date`), `GET /api/maintenance/analytics/:type/export`, `DELETE /api/maintenance/subscriptions/unconfirmed`, with `settings:maintain`, upstream's parameters, envelopes and validation.
4. **Work size:** these can touch millions of rows. Work in short batches so the single writer stays available and other traffic keeps moving, and bound the export by streaming it. A request that would run long behaves as upstream's client expects, or has a stated difference.
5. **Consent and privacy:** deleting subscribers goes through the subscriber owner, so D25 clearing, D61 webhooks and the bulk delivery budget apply. Say what a maintenance delete does when it crosses the budget. Sends and analytics history follow the retention rules in 03. Exports contain only what upstream's does, and `individual_tracking` off keeps subscriber IDs out.
6. **Native tables:** say whether maintenance should cover this project's own retained tables, such as delivered `webhook_deliveries`, expired sessions and `bounce_receipts`. Do the small ones if they fit the same screen, otherwise leave them on the backlog with a reason.
