## Tests (new `test/e2e/specs/sequences-lifecycle.e2e.js`, about 10 tests)

Use real time; the server has no clock control. Keep waits at `PT0S` or the smallest that proves the point, read the scheduler interval from code, and bound every wait. Assert on the sink and the API/explorer.

1. Re-entry `never`: a second trigger for the same person while enrolled or after exit is refused (409 message) or ignored, as the product defines; `after_exit`: refused while enrolled, accepted after exit; `always`: accepted while enrolled. Check through the explorer or API.
2. An event-triggered sequence with a `PT0S` first step sends its mail within one scheduler interval of `POST /api/subscriber-events`.
3. A trigger with a `where` clause: an event whose data matches enrolls; one that does not match enrolls nobody.
4. Pausing an active sequence stops a due step from sending (no mail within due time plus two ticks); setting it active again sends it.
5. Archiving (or the product's deactivate) stops new enrollments and sends, as the product defines.
6. A step condition false with `onFalse: skip` skips that step and the next one sends; with `onFalse: exit` the enrollment exits and nothing more sends.
7. A changed wait applied through the reschedule endpoint moves a waiting enrollment's due time, and the step sends at the new time (complements `sequences.e2e.js:410`, which checks the request only).
8. The timeline (`/api/enrollments/:id/timeline` and its page) shows sent, skipped and failed entries for a run that has each (a failed send through a spec-owned sink that refuses one address, as `operations.e2e.js` B66 does for auto-pause).

If a test reveals a real product bug, do not fix product code: mark it `test.fail()` naming the bug and report it prominently in the PR.
